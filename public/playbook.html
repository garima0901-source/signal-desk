From registry signal to qualified pipeline.

Why build outbound on signals

Firms that run deals buy tools when the workload is about to grow. A new fund means a deployment period is starting. A new acquisition stack means a deal is being signed, and the portfolio work comes next. A new firm or advisory boutique means a team setting up its tools for the first time.

The UK register records each of these events as soon as the entity is formed, usually before any press release. Reaching a firm in that window means a GTM Associate is contacting someone who has a reason to talk now, not a list name with no context.

The method is simple: signal → sponsor → persona → play. The register supplies the first step. The associate's research, done in a fixed way, supplies the rest.

Who we sell to

Buying roles at a mid-market sponsor or advisory boutique, what their week involves, and what each one needs to see.

Analyst / Associate

PE, VC, infrastructure, corporate finance
Their week
Screening information memoranda, building comps and market maps, first drafts of investment committee papers, pulling data from filings and data rooms.
Where AI helps
First drafts, document Q&A across a data room, comps and precedent pulls, every answer with a source they can click.
Role in the deal
Daily user and most likely champion.
Typical objection
“I can’t put a number in front of a VP unless I know where it came from.”
Proof they need
Cited output on a document they already know well.

VP / Principal

Runs the deal process
Their week
Running live processes, reviewing junior work, managing advisers and lenders, preparing for investment committee.
Where AI helps
Faster turnaround on first drafts and less time spent reviewing them; more processes covered per team.
Role in the deal
Influencer and often the person who sponsors a pilot.
Typical objection
“Doing the work is how juniors learn.”
Proof they need
Review time saved on a live workstream, with quality unchanged.

Partner / MD

Origination and investment committee
Their week
Sourcing, relationships, investment committee decisions, fundraising and LP updates.
Where AI helps
Covering more of the market with the same team; consistent, faster investment committee preparation.
Role in the deal
Economic buyer at most mid-market firms.
Typical objection
“Our deal data can’t leave our control.”
Proof they need
A clear security position and a peer firm’s experience.

COO / CFO / Head of Operations

Owns vendors, risk and budget
Their week
Fund operations, compliance, vendor management, IT and security.
Where AI helps
One governed tool instead of staff pasting deal data into general chatbots.
Role in the deal
Gatekeeper on the paper process and security review.
Typical objection
“We don’t have capacity for another security review.”
Proof they need
Security documentation, a data processing agreement, and access controls that respect information barriers.

Founding partner, new firm

Spin-outs and new managers
Their week
Raising the first fund, hiring, setting up operations, and still doing deals themselves.
Where AI helps
Research capacity of a larger firm before hiring the team to match.
Role in the deal
User, champion and buyer in one person, so cycles are short.
Typical objection
“We’re not ready yet; fund one first.”
Proof they need
Fast time to value on the fundraising and pipeline work they are doing right now.

Director, advisory boutique

Corporate finance, M&A, debt advisory
Their week
Pitches, information memoranda, buyer and lender lists, running sale processes with lean teams.
Where AI helps
Pitch and memorandum drafting, buyer universe research, process Q&A.
Role in the deal
Buyer and user; the firm’s founder often signs.
Typical objection
“Our clients expect partner-level judgement, not software.”
Proof they need
The same partner judgement with more of the groundwork done for them.

Plays: signal → angle → sequence

One play per signal type. The messages are templates, and anything in [brackets] is filled in from the associate's research.

Ground rule: never open with “I saw your Companies House filing.” The filing is a reason to look, not something to say. Reference a fund or deal by name only once it is public on the firm’s website or in the press. Otherwise, the timing does the work and the message stays about the buyer’s workflow.

Team operating system

How a GTM Associate team runs: the weekly rhythm, the research standard, onboarding new associates, and how calls are reviewed.

Weekly rhythm

  • Monday: signal triage. New register entries (monthly) and news are split up by tier and territory; check for duplicates against the CRM.
  • Tuesday–Thursday: protected research and sequencing blocks in the morning, calls in the afternoon.
  • Thursday: marketing sync on campaigns and inbound routing.
  • Friday: one recorded call reviewed as a team, pipeline review against exit criteria, and next week's targets agreed.

Signal-to-sequence standard

  1. Open the entity on Companies House; note officers and persons with significant control.
  2. Identify the sponsor firm and match it against the CRM.
  3. Tier it with the rules above and pick the play.
  4. Find 2–3 people across personas, not just one.
  5. Write a first line about their workflow, not the filing.
  6. Log the signal type, date and source URL on the account.

Onboarding: first 8 weeks

  • Weeks 1–2: product certification; learn the deal process end to end (sourcing → screening → diligence → investment committee → closing → 100-day plan); shadow 10 calls; build 20 accounts from the feed.
  • Weeks 3–4: first sequences live; manager reviews every first line before sending; first two discovery calls with manager support.
  • Weeks 5–6: full weekly sequencing target; runs discovery calls alone; call reviews twice a week.
  • Weeks 7–8: qualification certification (a mock call scored on the rubric); handoffs to account executives without manager review.

Call review rubric

OpeningGives a specific, workflow-based reason for the call within 20 seconds.
DiscoveryLearns the workflow, volumes and who is involved before pitching.
QualificationCaptures the handoff fields (Metrics, Pain, Champion, Decision criteria).
ObjectionsHandles security and accuracy questions with specifics, not reassurance.
Next stepEnds with a date, attendees and an agenda.

Scored 1–4 on each. A call that scores 15 or more out of 20 goes into the team's library of good examples.

30 / 60 / 90 days as GTM Manager

Days 1–30 · Learn and measure

  • Ride along on calls with every associate and two account executives; take the product certification.
  • Audit the CRM against the required fields; set the baseline for each scorecard metric.
  • Map current outbound: sequences, reply rates by message, and where the pipeline comes from.
  • Run the signal feed alongside existing lists as a test with one associate.

Days 31–60 · Standardise

  • Roll out the signal-to-sequence standard, the call review rubric and the stage exit criteria.
  • Rebuild sequences by play; retire messages with the lowest reply rates.
  • Agree the qualification bar and handoff standard with the account executives.
  • Set up the marketing sync and inbound routing rules.

Days 61–90 · Scale

  • Set scorecard targets from the baseline; weekly reviews run on the dashboard.
  • Write the onboarding plan and hiring profile for the next associates.
  • Report pipeline by signal type and keep investing where it converts.
  • Extend coverage beyond London and plan EMEA expansion.
